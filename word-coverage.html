How words are judged

For each word, every included trial at each level is pooled across participants. A 4AFC logistic is fitted by maximum likelihood: chance fixed at 25%, upper limit free between 70% and 100% (so a word that plateaus is not forced to 100%). SNR is fitted in dB; cutoff in octaves (log2 Hz).

The 62.5% point is the level giving 62.5% correct (halfway from chance to 100%, the adaptive target). Its 95% interval comes from resampling participants 200 times. With 10–20 participants these intervals run slightly narrow (in simulation they held the true value about 90% of the time), so treat them as a guide.

A word is covered when its function is no more than 20% of the way up at the hardest level tested and at least 80% of the way up at the easiest. Otherwise it needs lower levels (floor not reached), higher levels (top not reached), or both. Low ceiling: the curve levels off within the tested range at below 86% correct, which points to the recording or the foils rather than the levels. With about 10–20 trials per point this is a prompt to listen to the word, not proof; a plateau that sits right at the easiest level tested can also be missed. Too little data: fewer than 30 trials.

The suggested range for a word runs from 10% to 90% of the way up its function (never below 75 Hz, the app's lowest cutoff). A word whose span is more than three times the typical span is marked too flat to judge. The pooled curve includes differences between listeners, so it is shallower than any one listener's curve, and the ranges are on the generous side. Range needed uses where words' 62.5% points fall, widened at each end by a typical word's spread.
